# TARNEEB AI — COMPLETE JAWAKER-INSPIRED GAME APPLICATION

## 1. Main objective

Design and build a **fully interactive, visually polished, Arabic-first Tarneeb card game application**.

The game's visual experience, table organization, card interactions, bidding controls, animations, and overall playing flow should be strongly inspired by the familiar experience of **Jawaker's Tarneeb game**.

The most important requirement:

**When someone opens this application, the experience should immediately feel familiar to a person who regularly plays Tarneeb on Jawaker.**

This means:

- A compact four-player card table.
- A human player positioned at the bottom.
- Their partner directly opposite at the top.
- Two opponents on the left and right.
- A clearly visible, horizontally overlapping 13-card hand.
- Facedown cards behind the other players.
- Player avatars and name labels around the table.
- Quick bidding and pass interactions.
- Direct card-playing interactions with minimal clicks.
- Four cards appearing naturally in the middle of the table.
- Quick, readable trick resolution.
- Compact score and game-status indicators.
- Fast, continuous gameplay with minimal interruptions.

The game must not look like a dashboard, educational interface, chess application, casino website, or generic web app.

**It must look and behave like a dedicated multiplayer-style Tarneeb game, even though the other three players are AI.**

### Important design-reference rule

Use Jawaker as the primary **interaction and layout inspiration**, not as a source of artwork to copy.

Do not reproduce Jawaker's logo, original avatars, proprietary graphics, card-back artwork, branded icons, or identical visual assets.

Create an original design that gives players the same familiar table-first experience.

Reference sources:

- Jawaker official app: https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.boundless.jawaker
- Jawaker Tarneeb rules: https://blog.jawaker.com/en/tarneeb-rules-en/

If screenshots are supplied, study their component positions, spacing, scale, interaction patterns, hand arrangement, player placement, and table proportions.

Do not blindly copy colors or decorative details.

# 4. UX priority: Make gameplay feel like Jawaker

This is the highest-priority section.

## 4.1 The game table is the entire experience

Do not build a page with a navbar, sidebar, multiple rectangular dashboard panels, and a small embedded game.

The gameplay screen should feel like a full-screen card table.

Most of the available screen should be devoted to the four players, the table, and the human's cards.

Secondary controls should be small, unobtrusive, and placed near screen edges.

No scrolling during ordinary gameplay.

## 4.2 Compact player positioning

Show three AI players positioned:

- Top center: Your AI partner.
- Left center: AI opponent.
- Right center: AI opponent.

The human player occupies the bottom.

Each AI player should have:

- Circular avatar.
- Name label.
- Small team indicator.
- Facedown card fan.
- Active-turn indicator.
- Bid/pass bubble when relevant.

The avatars should remain at consistent locations throughout the game.

Players should look like seated participants, not like cards in a dashboard.

## 4.3 Human cards at the bottom

The human hand should be displayed as a **single horizontally overlapping row of 13 cards**, similar to the familiar Jawaker hand.

Do not display human cards in a large curved semicircle that wastes half the viewport.

Do not place the cards in separate boxes or inventory slots.

Arrange them tightly so the rank and suit of every card remain visible.

The hand should span most of the width of the game area.

When the human has fewer cards, redistribute the spacing naturally.

## 4.4 Fast card interaction

The player should be able to play a card in one simple action.

Default behavior:

- Hovering highlights or slightly raises a card.
- Clicking or tapping a legal card plays it immediately.
- The card animates to the middle.
- The game proceeds automatically.

Do not require a separate Play Card confirmation button for every card.

Allow an optional two-step confirmation setting for players who prefer it.

If a card is illegal, it must not be played. Explain the reason subtly when useful.

Prevent double-clicking from sending duplicate actions.

## 4.5 Fast bidding

When the human needs to bid, show a compact, clearly readable bidding control near the lower-center of the game area.

Use large, touch-friendly numeric choices and a distinct **باس** button.

Bidding should feel quick and natural.

The human must not navigate to a separate screen to bid.

Do not display an enormous central modal that hides their entire hand.

Show short, readable bid/pass announcements near each player's avatar.

## 4.6 Automatic flow

AI turns should proceed automatically.

The user should not have to press:

- Continue Turn.
- Next Player.
- Resolve Trick.
- Confirm AI Move.

AI actions, trick resolution, and transitions should happen naturally.

Use short animations to maintain readability, not artificial delays to make the game look complex.

# 11. Card-play experience

During trick play, each player plays exactly one card into the center of the table.

### Card placement

The central trick should have four relative positions:

- Top card: AI partner.
- Right card: right opponent.
- Bottom card: human.
- Left card: left opponent.

When each player acts:

1. Animate the selected card from the hand or seat.
2. Move it toward the player's central trick position.
3. Display it face-up.
4. Leave it visible until the trick is resolved.

Do not arrange all four cards in a horizontal row.

Use a compact four-direction arrangement like a real digital Tarneeb table.

### Trick resolution

After the fourth card:

- Briefly highlight the winning card.
- Identify the winning seat.
- Animate the cards collecting toward the winner.
- Increase the winning team's trick count.
- Begin the next trick.

Use subtle, fast motion.

The next trick is led by the previous trick's winner.

### Gameplay pacing

The player should feel the same fast rhythm as a familiar online card game.

Suggested durations:

- AI action transition: 200–400 ms.
- Card movement: 180–300 ms.
- Trick-winner pause: 500–750 ms.
- Trick cleanup: 180–300 ms.

Use no artificial waiting after an action if the next state is ready.

Respect reduced-motion settings.

# 17. Real game rules — immutable

The interface must follow the existing research version:

**tarneeb-classic-v1**

Jawaker is a UX inspiration. It is not allowed to override game mechanics.

### Players

Four players.

Partners: seats 0 and 2; seats 1 and 3.

Turn order: 0 → 1 → 2 → 3 → 0.

### Dealing

52 cards.

13 cards per player.

Ace high.

The dealer rotates to the right between scored deals.

First bidder:

`(dealer + 1) % 4`

### Auction

Bids: 7–13.

Bids must strictly increase.

Pass is permanent for the auction.

All-pass causes a redeal by the same dealer with no scoring.

Highest bidder becomes declarer.

### Trump

The declarer chooses clubs, diamonds, hearts or spades.

No-trump is disabled.

The declarer leads the first trick.

### Card play

Players must follow suit when possible.

The highest trump wins; otherwise the highest card of the led suit wins.

The trick winner leads the next trick.

Each deal contains 13 tricks.

### Scoring

Let:

- `b` = winning bid.
- `tB` = tricks won by the bidding team.
- `tD = 13 − tB`.

Apply these rules in exact precedence:

1. Bid 13 made with 13 tricks: bidding team +26, defenders 0.
2. Bid 13 failed: bidding team −16, defenders `2 × tD`.
3. Bid below 13 with all 13 tricks taken by bidding team: +16, defenders 0.
4. Bid below 13 made normally: bidding team `+tB`, defenders 0.
5. Bid below 13 with defending-team kaboot: bidding team `−b`, defenders +16.
6. Other failed bids: bidding team `−b`, defenders `+tD`.

Negative scores are allowed.

### Match end

Target: 31 points.

The match ends after scoring when a team reaches at least 31 points.

Do not add any unsupported regional options.

# 18. Correct AI integration

The original Tarneeb-RL project includes a Python engine and recurrent AI models.

The generated application must support future connection to that existing code.

Keep frontend gameplay presentation separate from authoritative game state.

Create a clear `GameAdapter` abstraction for:

- Starting matches.
- Retrieving player-visible state.
- Retrieving legal actions.
- Submitting human actions.
- Advancing AI turns.
- Reading public events.
- Reading current tricks.
- Reading scoring outcomes.
- Starting the next deal.
- Ending the match.

The adapter interface is a proposed integration boundary, not an existing API.

Do not fabricate a production backend.

### AI seat independence

Every AI seat must maintain its own:

- Observation.
- Recurrent hidden state.
- Public event cursor.
- Game-state lifecycle.

The model's recurrent state resets between deals and all-pass redeals as required by the Python implementation.

Never feed hidden cards into actor observations.

### Action space

Existing action IDs:

- 0–51: card actions.
- 52–58: bids 7–13.
- 59: Pass.
- 60–63: trump suits.
- 64: reserved no-trump action, always illegal.

Use the actual Python implementation as authority for card indexing and suit mapping.

Do not guess card IDs.

# 30. Final quality standard

The final app should feel like opening a familiar Arabic Tarneeb application and immediately sitting at a card table.

The main game screen must look like a real card game, not a web interface containing a card game.

The user should be able to:

1. Open the app.
2. Press **العب الآن**.
3. Receive 13 cards.
4. Bid or pass using compact controls.
5. Watch the other players bid.
6. Select trump if they become declarer.
7. Tap legal cards directly.
8. Watch cards move to the center.
9. See the trick winner and updated trick count.
10. Continue naturally through all 13 tricks.
11. See correct scoring.
12. Start the next deal.
13. Continue until a team reaches 31.
14. See a proper victory or defeat screen.

Every control must have a purpose.

Every visual element must support gameplay.

Every game rule must match `tarneeb-classic-v1`.

**Prioritize the familiar Jawaker-style playing experience over decorative design, while using a completely original visual identity.**

Build a functional application, not simply a beautiful mockup.
